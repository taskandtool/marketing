# Frameworks

Examples use a made-up two-van heating firm in Leeds.

## Awareness (Schwartz)

Copy cannot create desire, only channel it, and the opening depends on what
the reader already knows.

| Stage | Knows | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | nothing is wrong yet | a story or a surprising fact; never the product |
| Problem-aware | the pain | the pain in their words, and a hint it is solvable |
| Solution-aware | the category | why this way works and others don't |
| Product-aware | this business | objections answered; proof over promise |
| Most aware | wants it | the offer; explaining costs the sale |

Cold audiences are mostly the first three; people who have visited or
enquired are the last two.

## Market sophistication (Schwartz)

How many claims the market has already heard. Read it off competitors' ads.

1. First to say it: a simple, direct claim, shown.
2. Others echo it: a bigger, more specific claim.
3. Claims exhausted: a **mechanism**, how we do it differently.
4. Mechanisms copied: a bigger mechanism.
5. Nothing believed: **identification**, who the customer is or wants to be,
   local belonging.

Local trades usually sit at 3 or 4: every van says "fast, friendly,
reliable". A bigger "same-day" loses; "the engineer who fitted it services
it" (mechanism) or "family firm, Leeds since 2004, we answer our own phone"
(identification) wins.

## Problem, agitate, solve

For problem-aware readers and short cold copy. Agitate with the real cost
(a cold house, a call-out fee), not dread.

> Cold radiators at the top? That's air, and it's costing you heat. Our
> engineers bleed and balance the whole system in one visit.

## Before, after, bridge

For solution-aware readers and before/after jobs: touch the pain without
dwelling on it.

> Before: black water from the bleed valve. After: every radiator hot in
> twenty minutes. The bridge: a power flush, done in a morning.

## StoryBrand

The customer is the hero; the business is the guide. A character wants
something, meets a problem (outside: the boiler is dead; inside: feeling
helpless; principle: a family shouldn't go cold because a firm won't answer
the phone), meets a guide with empathy and authority, gets a three-step
plan and a direct call to action, sees what failure costs and what success
looks like. Use it as the brand's message spine across many pieces, not as
the structure of one ad. If a draft makes the business the hero, rewrite it.

## Jobs to be done

People hire a product to make progress in a circumstance, and the
competition is whatever else does the job. The job of a January breakdown
call is "get the house warm before the kids are home, and don't get ripped
off"; the rival is a fan heater and a video, not just another firm. Ideas
come from the circumstance, not the service list.

## Objections

List five to eight, each with its source (lost enquiries, competitors'
one-star reviews, People Also Ask). Each becomes an idea for warm readers:
the confession ("we're not the cheapest"), the question answered, the
comparison, the guarantee. Cold readers get problems, not objections.

## Feature to benefit

Ask "so what?" twice to turn a feature into what it does for the reader.
Use it to sharpen one claim, not to structure a piece.

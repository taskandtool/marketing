# A show and a newsletter

Run a video podcast that interviews the industry's experts and the
business's own buyers, and send a weekly newsletter from it. Asking a
buyer onto a show is the easiest yes in sales, and in a year the business
owns an audience and a name in its niche.

## Fits

Sells to businesses in a niche with people worth hearing from, and an owner
willing to host every week for a year. Slow: judged at twelve months.

## What it looks like

- An episode a week, recorded remotely (Riverside), guests drawn from the
  people the business wants as customers.
- A newsletter each week built on the episode: the best ideas, written to
  stand alone.
- The list grown from cold email (`cold-email.md`) and every other channel.
- Each episode cut into many clips and written posts: one insight each,
  rewritten per platform.
- The best episodes turned into a lead magnet: a checklist, a swipe file.

## Tools

- Transcripts: the recording tool's own, or a transcription service
  (Deepgram).
- Posts from an episode: the `social-post` skill and its repurpose
  reference; clips from the transcript's timestamps, cut by the owner's
  editor or a clipping tool.
- The newsletter: the `email` skill, sent from the owner's email tool.
- Guests: research and the outreach email, as `podcast-guesting.md` does
  in reverse.

## Watch

Newsletter subscribers and open rate, episode views, and how many guests
became conversations about buying.

## Repeats

From each new recording: transcript, newsletter draft, a week of posts and
a list of clip timestamps, ready for the owner.

## Gotchas

- A guest's words in an ad need their consent; on the show and in clips
  they agreed to.
- The newsletter goes only to people who signed up or were told they would
  get it.

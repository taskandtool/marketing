# Hooks

A hook does two jobs at once: it interrupts (motion, an odd object, a face
mid-sentence, a surprising line) and it signals relevance (this is for
someone like me: the house type, the problem, the place). Interrupt alone is
clickbait; relevance alone is invisible.

## Writing one

1. Read the idea's angle and awareness stage in its `creative.md`, and
   `research/audience.md` for the customers' own words.
2. Pick the hook type that fits the stage (`references/catalogue.md`).
   Problem-aware readers get the problem in their words; solution-aware
   readers get a comparison; product-aware readers get proof or the
   objection answered.
3. Write five to eight versions of the opening line, each a different type
   or a different angle into the same type, using the customer's words over
   the business's.
4. Test each against the rules below and keep the best two. Record the
   others in `research/hooks.md` for later.

## Rules

- The proposition lands in the first three seconds of video and the first
  line of copy; the payoff by about six seconds. A hook the body does not
  pay off gets a good hook rate and a bad hold rate.
- Frame one is mid-action: the product in hand, the result, the problem in
  progress. Never a logo, an establishing shot or a fade.
- It works muted: the picture and the on-screen words carry it.
- It looks like something a person would post. Heavy branding, perfect
  lighting, a blank background or scripted delivery in the first frame get
  skipped.
- Callouts name the situation, never the person's age, health, money or
  other personal attributes; Meta refuses those.
- A comparison never names a competitor, and every claim in it is in
  `claims.md`.

## On a winning ad

Keep the body and write five to eight new hooks for it before changing
anything else. Then the same angle in a new format. A losing ad changes
angle, not colour.

# Ad ideas

An idea is one argument for one reader at one stage of awareness. The best
come from evidence (the sources below). For a service business, start with
three ideas: the outcome, social proof and the problem. With an offer, make
one of the three the offer.

## Steps

Copy and tick:

- [ ] 1. Read `brand/positioning.md`, `brand/audience.md`, `claims.md` and
      `research/brief.md`. With no research yet, say so and offer it; ideas
      without it are guesses.
- [ ] 2. Decide the market's sophistication stage
      (`references/frameworks.md`). If every competitor makes the same claim,
      a bigger version of it will not stand out. Lead with how this
      business does the work, or who it is for.
- [ ] 3. Write ten to fifteen candidate ideas from the sources below.
      Each is one line: reader, stage, argument and its proof in
      `claims.md`.
- [ ] 4. Score each (below). Keep the top scorers that make different
      arguments: as many as the owner asked for, or else three for a test
      set (the `ad` skill).
- [ ] 5. For each kept idea, create `creatives/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>/creative.md`
      with `status: draft`, `angle`, `awareness`, `framework`, `claims`,
      and the idea in two sentences under `## Idea`. The hook comes next (the `copywriting` skill).
- [ ] 6. Show the owner the kept ideas as a short list, one line each, and ask
      which to make first.

An idea as a line:

Weak: "Show people we're reliable and experienced." <!-- tropes-allow -->

Kept: "Owners of a boiler over ten years old, unsure whether to fix it | problem-aware | the engineer who fitted it tells you straight whether it's worth repairing | 'same two engineers since 2004' (claims.md)"

The weak one has no reader, no stage and nothing to prove it.

## Where ideas come from

- **Customers' words**: their wants, dislikes and worries (`research/audience.md`).
- **Objections**: price, trust, timing, mess, "can I do it myself", "will
  they turn up". Each is an idea: the confession, the question answered,
  the comparison, the guarantee.
- **The circumstance**: what the customer is trying to get done and what
  else they might use instead (a fan heater, a how-to video or another firm).
- **Competitor gaps**: the objection nobody answers, the angle nobody runs.
- **Proof the business has**: a real job, a number, a review.
- **The calendar**: the first cold week, a local event, a season.

## Scoring, before spending

Score each idea 0 to 2 on each of these:

- Specific to this business: a competitor could not run it unchanged.
- The proof is in `claims.md`.
- It fits the reader's stage.
- It isn't a weaker version of a competitor's claim.
- A person could have posted it.
- It differs from the others in the set.
- It can be made with what the business has.

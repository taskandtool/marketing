# Frameworks

Examples use a made-up two-van heating firm in Leeds.

## Awareness (Schwartz)

You cannot create desire with words, only channel one the reader already has. Choose the opening
by what the reader already knows.

| Stage | Knows | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | nothing is wrong yet | a story or a surprising fact; never the product |
| Problem-aware | the pain | the pain in their words, and a hint it is solvable |
| Solution-aware | the category | why this way works and others don't |
| Product-aware | this business | objections answered; proof over promise |
| Most aware | wants it | the offer, with no explanation |

Cold audiences are mostly the first three; people who have visited or
enquired are the last two.

## Market sophistication (Schwartz)

How many claims the market has already heard. Judge it from competitors' ads.

1. First to say it: a simple, direct claim, shown.
2. Others echo it: a bigger, more specific claim.
3. Claims exhausted: a **mechanism**, how we do it differently.
4. Mechanisms copied: a bigger mechanism.
5. Nothing believed: **identification**, who the customer is or wants to be,
   local belonging.

Local trades are usually at 3 or 4: every van says "fast, friendly,
reliable". A bigger "same-day" claim will not stand out. Use a mechanism
("the engineer who fitted it services it") or identification ("family
firm, Leeds since 2004, we answer our own phone").

## Problem, agitate, solve

For problem-aware readers and short cold copy. Agitate with the real cost
(a cold house, a call-out fee), not dread.

> Cold radiators at the top? That's air, and it's costing you heat. Our
> engineers bleed and balance the whole system in one visit.

## Before, after, bridge

For solution-aware readers and before/after jobs: touch the pain without
dwelling on it.

> Before: black water from the bleed valve. After: every radiator hot in
> twenty minutes. The bridge: a power flush in one morning.

## StoryBrand

The customer is the hero and the business is the guide. A character wants
something and meets a problem on three levels: outside (the boiler is
dead), inside (feeling helpless) and principle (a family shouldn't go cold
because a firm won't answer the phone). They meet a guide with empathy and
authority, who gives them a three-step plan and a direct call to action.
They see what failure costs and what success looks like. Use it as
the message behind all of the brand's pieces, not as the structure of one
ad. If a draft makes the business the hero, rewrite it.

## Jobs to be done

People hire a product to make progress in a circumstance. The competition
is anything else they could use for that job. The job of a January breakdown
call is "get the house warm before the kids are home, and don't get ripped
off". The rival is a fan heater and a video, not just another firm. Draw
ideas from the circumstance, not the service list.

## Objections

List five to eight, each with its source (lost enquiries, competitors'
one-star reviews, People Also Ask). Turn each into an idea for warm
readers: the confession ("we're not the cheapest"), the question answered,
the comparison, the guarantee. For cold readers, use problems instead.

## Feature to benefit

Ask "so what?" twice to turn a feature into what it does for the reader.
Use it to sharpen one claim, not to structure a piece.

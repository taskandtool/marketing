# Pinterest organic pins

Recipes for a business account. Pinterest is a search engine with a long tail: a pin from last year still brings visits. It suits any business whose work can be photographed and planned for (kitchens, gardens, weddings, food, interiors, clothes). The worked examples use Harlow Joinery, a made-up workshop in Bristol that makes fitted kitchens, run by Jo and Sam. Numbers in examples are illustrative; a real pin takes its figures from the claims ledger.

## What performs

- Pinterest's own creative guidance: "We recommend using a 2:3 aspect ratio, or 1000 x 1500 pixels". Pins taller than 2:3 "might get cut off".
- Layout, from Pinterest: "Stack your story by putting visuals in the middle, then stacking text overlay with key messaging at the top and extra details at the bottom". Show products "in realistic settings".
- Hootsuite: keywords in the title, the description, the board name and on the destination page. Fresh pins (a new image, even to the same URL) beat re-pins of an existing image.
- Pins are found by search months later, so evergreen subjects beat news. There is no share-signal data for Pinterest in the report; saves to boards are the measure that matters.

## Norms

- Every pin is 2:3, 1000 x 1500 or larger. Vertical only.
- Title: keyword first, inside the part that shows (limits: `specs/pinterest.md`).
- Description: one or two sentences with the natural search phrase ("fitted kitchen with an alcove, Bristol"), no hashtags needed, no calls to action shouted.
- Hashtags: none, or one or two at the end. Keywords do the work.
- Links: native. Every pin links to the matching page on your site: the project page, the service page, the guide. Never the homepage for everything.
- Alt text: Pinterest has a field for it. One literal sentence with the keyword.
- Boards: named for what people search ("Fitted kitchens Bristol", "Small kitchen layouts"), not for the business ("Our work").
- Cadence for one person: at least one fresh pin a week. Batch them: five new images from one finished job give five weeks of pins to the same project page.

## Recipes

### Finished work pin
When: after every project with a photo worth planning around. The proof pillar, and the easiest fresh pin.
Structure: one photo of the finished work in the middle two thirds, a 3 to 6 word overlay in the top third, the business name small at the bottom. Links to that project's page.
Copy: title "Alcove kitchen in oak, Bristol". Description: "A fitted kitchen built into a chimney alcove in a Redland terrace. Oak frame-and-panel doors, oiled oak worktop, made in our Bristol workshop." Overlay: "Kitchen built into the alcove".
Visual: 1000 x 1500, the room in daylight, no people needed, the overlay in brand type and colour.
Pitfalls: a square Instagram crop, the overlay over the interesting part of the photo, a link to the homepage.

### Guide pin
When: the education pillar, once a month. A pin that answers a search question and links to the guide or FAQ page that answers it fully.
Structure: the question or the number as the overlay in the top third, one photo or diagram in the middle, three short bullet lines at the bottom.
Copy: title "How to measure a kitchen for a quote". Description: "The three measurements a joiner needs before pricing a fitted kitchen, and why the walls are never straight. From a Bristol kitchen workshop." Bottom lines: "Wall to wall at three heights. Ceiling at four corners. Window and door reveals."
Visual: a photo of a tape on a wall, or a simple line diagram of a room, in the brand palette.
Pitfalls: the whole guide on the pin so nobody clicks, a stock diagram, a title that starts with the business name.

### Idea pin for a service
When: the offer pillar, at most one pin in five. A pin that leads to the service page or the booking page.
Structure: the outcome as the overlay, the work as the image, the service and area in the description, a link to the service page.
Copy: title "Fitted kitchens made in Bristol". Description: "Workshop-made kitchens measured and fitted by the same joiner. Eight weeks from signed drawing to fit day. Book a design visit on the page." Overlay: "Measured by the joiner who fits it".
Visual: the workshop bench with a kitchen carcass on it, or a fitted kitchen with the maker in it.
Pitfalls: a price on the pin that will go stale, a discount with a deadline (pins live for years), a graphic with no photo.

## Never

- Horizontal or square pins, or a pin taller than 2:3.
- Re-pinning the same image again and again instead of making a fresh one.
- Stock photos, or a product cut out on white with no room around it.
- Watermarked reposts of other people's pins as your own, or a customer's photo without permission.
- A hashtag wall, or a title stuffed with keywords ("Kitchen kitchens fitted kitchen Bristol kitchen design").
- Dated offers and prices on a pin that will still be found in two years.
- A link to the homepage for every pin, or a link to a page that does not match the image.
- The `tropes` skill's refused phrases: "Welcome to", "Here's the thing", "Whether you're X or Y", "Say goodbye to", "game-changer", "We're passionate about", "Let's dive in", "solutions" as the noun, the "It's not X. It's Y." pivot, the "Fast. Simple. Effective." tricolon, em dashes, invented proof.

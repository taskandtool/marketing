# Facebook Page organic posts

Recipes for a business Page. The worked examples use Harlow Joinery, a made-up workshop in Bristol that makes fitted kitchens, run by Jo and Sam. Numbers in examples are illustrative; a real post takes its figures from the claims ledger.

## What performs

- Reach rate by format (Socialinsider, Aug 2026, 872k posts): images 1.20%, albums 1.15%, status or text 0.80%, Reels 0.55%, links 0.20%. Hootsuite (Feb 2025): albums lead engagement at 1.6%.
- So: photos and albums first, plain text for conversation, Reels for reach beyond followers, links almost never in the body.
- Links: Buffer (Jun 2026): "97.3% of all post views on Facebook in the US go to updates that don't include a link to a source outside of the app". Buffer's advice: "try dropping the link in the first comment."
- Length: Hootsuite: "Posts with 80 characters or fewer receive 66% higher engagement".
- Ranking (Meta transparency centre): likes and comments as totals and as a ratio to views, content type, originality ("the percentage of identical content between this post and other posts"), and predicted angry reactions and reports, which demote. Engagement bait such as "tag a friend" and "like if" is demoted (Meta, Hootsuite).
- Shares and sends are heavily weighted, as on Instagram. Write the post so one person would forward it to one other person.

## Norms

- Limits: `specs/meta.md`. Rule: the whole point in the first two lines, under 80 characters where possible.
- Hooks: a specific local fact, a photo that shows the change, or one real question.
- Hashtags: none, or one.
- Links: first comment, or the Page's About section. In the body only when the link itself is the point.
- Alt text: on every photo, one literal sentence with the service and place.
- Local tools: Events for anything with a date. Groups: post as the Page where allowed, answer questions rather than advertise. Reviews: ask for them, reply to every one. Keep hours and address current.
- Cadence for one person: two or three posts a week "for at least three months" (Buffer). Reply to comments the same day.

## Recipes

### Photo and short story
When: the default post. Proof, behind the scenes or community, two of the week's three posts.
Structure: 1 to 4 real photos. A hook of 80 characters or fewer, one to three short lines, a question at the end.
Copy: "Ruth's alcove kitchen in Redland, fitted Thursday. Every showroom wanted to board the alcove over. We built the run into it instead. Would you have kept the alcove or lost it for a straight run?"
Visual: phone photos from the job, one of them with a person in it.
Pitfalls: stock imagery, text on the image that makes it look like an ad, a question nobody would answer.

### Album / job recap
When: the end of every project. The highest-reach format.
Structure: 5 to 10 photos of one job, the caption narrating start to finish.
Copy: "Bedminster, March to May. Photo 1 is the room on survey day. 2 and 3 are the carcasses in the workshop. 4 to 7 are fit week. 8 is Sunday lunch, sent by the owners. Eight weeks from drawing to fit, ash fronts, oak worktop."
Visual: photos in the order the job happened, the finished kitchen as the cover.
Pitfalls: ten near-identical shots, no people, the client not asked.

### Text-only question or announcement
When: when there is one specific local question, or news with no photo yet. Conversation, not reach.
Structure: one or two lines, no image, no link.
Copy: "Fitting in Southville next week. Anyone on the Cotswold Road side who has parking permits, how strict is the warden on trade vans?"
Visual: none.
Pitfalls: engagement bait ("like if you agree"), a question with an obvious answer, an announcement that should have been an Event.

### Event
When: anything with a date: an open workshop, a showroom weekend, a talk.
Structure: create the Event first. Then a photo post that describes it and links the Event in the first comment. Repost a week before and the day before.
Copy: "Workshop open day, Saturday 18 October, 10 to 3, Bedminster. Come and see a kitchen being built before it is fitted. Kids welcome, tea on. Details and the map in the first comment."
Visual: a photo of the workshop with people in it from last time, or of the bench.
Pitfalls: the date only in the image, the Event without a follow-up post, a link in the body.

### Offer
When: no more than one post in five.
Structure: an image of the thing, the offer and deadline in the first line, then how to claim ("show this post" works well locally).
Copy: "Free design visit for kitchens booked before 31 October. Two hours in your house with Jo and a measured drawing you keep. Message the Page or mention this post when you call."
Visual: the thing on offer in a real home.
Pitfalls: urgency stacking, a discount every week, a link in the body.

### Review share
When: every review, on Facebook or elsewhere.
Structure: a screenshot of the review, thanks by name, one line of story.
Copy: "'The drawing was the kitchen. Nothing changed on fit day.' Thank you, Ruth. Fitted 14 August, the date we put on the drawing in June."
Visual: the real screenshot with the platform name visible.
Pitfalls: editing the review, a run of reviews with nothing else between them.

### Reel
When: any vertical video you already made. Reach to non-followers.
Structure: upload natively, the hook on screen in the first three seconds, a one-line caption.
Copy: "How a kitchen closes flush on a wall that leans 12 mm. Fitted kitchens, Bristol."
Visual: 9:16, captions on, no other app's watermark.
Pitfalls: a shared link to the Reel instead of the file, TikTok's logo in the corner.

### Link post
When: only when the link is the point (a news article about you, a booking page for a full event).
Structure: a photo post with the link in the first comment, not a link card.
Copy: "Bristol Post wrote about the four-day week at the workshop. Sam explains why we tried it. Link in the first comment."
Visual: a photo, not the link's preview card.
Pitfalls: the link in the body, a preview card with a stock image.

## Never

- Engagement bait: "tag a friend", "like if you agree", "comment YES", "share to win".
- A link in the body of a post that is not a link post.
- Stock imagery, or text on an image that looks like an ad.
- Hashtag walls, or the Instagram caption pasted in with the tags still on it.
- Reposts with another app's watermark, or a customer's content without permission.
- Angry-reaction bait: local politics, complaints about named competitors, arguments in the comments.
- The `tropes` skill's refused phrases: "Welcome to", "Here's the thing", "Whether you're X or Y", "Say goodbye to", "game-changer", "We're passionate about", "Let's dive in", "solutions" as the noun, the "It's not X. It's Y." pivot, the "Fast. Simple. Effective." tricolon, em dashes, invented proof.

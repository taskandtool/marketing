# X and Threads organic posts

Two text-first platforms in one file. For a one-person business they are optional: post only if it costs nothing, otherwise skip both. The worked examples use Harlow Joinery, a made-up workshop in Bristol that makes fitted kitchens, run by Jo and Sam. Numbers in examples are illustrative; a real post takes its figures from `claims.md`.

## What performs

X
- Weights (via Sprout): a reply is worth about 13.5 likes, a repost about 20, a bookmark about 10. Links cut reach by "50-90%" (Musk, via Sprout). A post loses half its visibility every six hours. Media beats plain text.
- Hootsuite: posts under 100 characters get about 17% more engagement; quote posts get about twice the engagement of plain reposts.
- Threads on X (Buffer): only when the content is genuinely multi-step. The first post must stand alone; number the rest.
- Sprout and Hootsuite for small businesses: one-line observations, a photo of the work, replies to local accounts. Two or three posts a day only if free; otherwise skip.

Threads (Meta)
- Ranking on replies, profile visits, repeat interaction with the same account, and timeliness (Buffer, Jan 2026). Replying to your own comments lifts engagement by 42% (Buffer).
- Hootsuite: under 200 characters. One topic tag per post. Answer every reply.
- Shares and sends are weighted as on Instagram. Write the line one person would send to one other person.

## Norms

- X: 280 characters (25,000 on Premium; do not use the longer limit). Under 100 is the target. Threads: 500 characters, under 200 the target.
- Hooks: the observation itself, or the photo. No "thread:" announcements, no "a few thoughts on".
- Hashtags: none on X. One topic tag on Threads.
- Links: never in the body on either. First reply, or the profile.
- Alt text: on every image, one literal sentence. Both platforms show it to screen readers.
- Cadence for one person: X two or three a day if free, else none. Threads one a day if free, else none. Neither replaces Instagram, Facebook or GBP.

## Recipes

### One-line observation (X and Threads)
When: something from the day's work that a homeowner or another trade would recognise.
Structure: one sentence, under 100 characters on X, under 200 on Threads. No tag on X, one topic tag on Threads.
Copy: "Every 'straight' wall we measured this month was out by 8 to 14 mm. The kitchen has to be cut to that, not to the drawing."
Visual: none, or one phone photo of the tape on the wall.
Pitfalls: a moral attached to the observation, a question added to farm replies.

### Photo of the work (X and Threads)
When: a finished detail, a stage of a build, a tool in use. Media beats text on both.
Structure: one photo, one line, alt text.
Copy: "Scribed end panel meeting a 1900s wall in Redland. The gap you cannot see took two hours." Alt text: "An oak kitchen end panel fitted flush to an uneven plaster wall."
Visual: a single phone photo, cropped tight on the detail.
Pitfalls: four photos where one would do, a watermark, a stock shot.

### Reply to a local account (X)
When: daily if you are on X at all. This is where a small account's reach comes from.
Structure: a useful reply to a local business, a neighbourhood account or a homeowner asking a question. No pitch.
Copy: replying to a Bristol homeowner asking about kitchen timelines: "Eight weeks from signed drawing to fit is realistic for a workshop-made kitchen. Anything under six usually means the units are stock and cut down on site."
Visual: none.
Pitfalls: replying with a link, pitching the business, replying to arguments.

### Thread (X only)
When: genuinely multi-step content. A checklist, a job told in stages. Rarely.
Structure: post 1 stands alone and states the payoff. Posts numbered 2/, 3/ and so on, one step each, a photo on most. Last post a plain wrap-up, link in a reply.
Copy: "1/ Three questions to ask before you sign a kitchen quote. 2/ Who measures the room, and is it the same person who fits it? 3/ Is the price from a drawing of your room or from a showroom range? 4/ What happens on fit day if the wall is not straight? 5/ Those are the three. Our checklist is in the reply below."
Visual: a photo on posts 2 to 4.
Pitfalls: a first post that is only a teaser, a thread of two, a thread every day.

### Threads post with a reply chain (Threads only)
When: daily post on Threads. Replies to your own post are weighted.
Structure: the post under 200 characters with one topic tag. Then one or two replies from you under it adding detail, and a reply to every person who answers.
Copy: post: "Showroom says the run is 3 metres. Tape says 2.94 and the wall leans. This is why a kitchen gets measured twice at Harlow. #kitchenrenovation" Own reply: "The second measure is on the day the carcasses are cut, from the same joiner who will fit them."
Visual: one phone photo on the post.
Pitfalls: leaving replies unanswered, more than one tag, a link in the post.

## Never

- A link in the body on either platform.
- Hashtags on X, or more than one topic tag on Threads. No hashtag walls.
- Reposts or screenshots of other people's posts with the watermark on, presented as your own.
- Reply farming: "Agree?", "RT if", "Who else?".
- Engagement pods, follow-for-follow, buying reach.
- Threads for the sake of threads, or a "thread:" opener.
- Arguments with local accounts, politics, complaints about named competitors.
- The `tropes` skill's refused phrases: "Welcome to", "Here's the thing", "Whether you're X or Y", "Say goodbye to", "game-changer", "We're passionate about", "Let's dive in", "solutions" as the noun, the "It's not X. It's Y." pivot, the "Fast. Simple. Effective." tricolon, em dashes, invented proof.

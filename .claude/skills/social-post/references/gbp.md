# Google Business Profile posts

Recipes for the three post types on a Business Profile: Updates, Offers and Events. GBP posts show on the business's Google listing and in Maps, to people already searching for the service in the area, so they are closer to a shop window than a feed. The worked examples use Harlow Joinery, a made-up workshop in Bristol that makes fitted kitchens, run by Jo and Sam. Numbers in examples are illustrative; a real post takes its figures from `claims.md`.

## What performs

- Sterling Sky (over 1,000 posts): Offers beat Events for clicks. Custom images get "5.6x more clicks" than stock. Posts with a title get about twice the clicks. Posts with emoji got about twice the clicks in their data. "Specials or discounts" averaged 6.04 clicks against 2.71 for plain calls to action.
- Hootsuite: "Google loves fresh activity"; a contractor in their example saw 10% more inquiries after posting job photos regularly.
- Google: "Posts older than 6 months are archived unless a date range is set". An Offer or Event with dates stays visible for its range.
- No share or save signal exists here. The measures are views, button clicks, calls and direction requests, read in the Profile's performance tab.

## Norms

- Post types (Google): an Update has a description, a photo or video, and an optional button. An Offer requires "a title, dates, and time" plus an optional description, photo, coupon code and terms. An Event needs "a title, start and end dates, and times".
- Length: the 1,500-character description and 58-character title limits are widely reported: unverified. Rule: a title-style first line under 58 characters, then two or three sentences.
- Google's content rules: no misspellings, no auto-generated text, no phone numbers in the copy. Use the button for calls.
- Photos: JPG or PNG, 10 KB to 5 MB, 720 x 720 recommended, 250 x 250 minimum, "in focus and well lit" with "no significant alterations". Video: 30 seconds or under, 75 MB or under, 720p or better.
- Hooks: the service and the area in the first line. People reading this are already searching for it.
- Hashtags: none. Links: the button only. Alt text: no field; the description carries the words.
- Cadence for one person: one Update a week. An Offer or Event whenever there is a real one. Photos added to the Profile as jobs finish, separately from posts.
- Keep hours, address, services and the Q&A section current. Reply to every review.

## Recipes

### Update
When: weekly. A finished job, a service explained, a piece of news.
Structure: one custom photo, a title-style first line, two or three sentences naming the service and the area, a "Learn more" or "Call now" button to the matching page.
Copy: "Alcove kitchen fitted in Redland. A workshop-made oak kitchen built into a chimney alcove, measured and fitted by the same joiner. We make fitted kitchens for homes across Bristol and North Somerset. Button: Learn more (the project page)."
Visual: one phone photo of the finished job, 720 x 720 or larger, no text overlay, no logo.
Pitfalls: a stock photo, a keyword-stuffed first line ("Fitted kitchens Bristol kitchen fitters Bristol"), a phone number in the text, a link pasted into the description.

### Offer
When: whenever there is a real offer with an end date. At most one live at a time. The highest-click type.
Structure: a clear title with the number in it, a date range, a coupon code if there is one, a one-line terms line, a photo of the thing.
Copy: "Title: Free design visit, worth £180, until 31 October. Description: Two hours in your home with Jo, and a measured drawing you keep whether or not you book the kitchen. Fitted kitchens across Bristol. Code: VISIT. Terms: Bristol and North Somerset postcodes, kitchens booked by 31 October 2026. Button: Book."
Visual: Jo at a kitchen table with a drawing, or the finished kitchen from a recent visit.
Pitfalls: no number in the title, an expired Offer still showing, terms that contradict the title, a discount every week.

### Event
When: anything with a date and a place: an open day, a showroom weekend, a talk at a local venue.
Structure: a title, start and end dates and times, one line saying who it is for, a "Book" or "Sign up" button.
Copy: "Title: Workshop open day, Bedminster. Saturday 18 October 2026, 10:00 to 15:00. For anyone planning a kitchen in the next year: see a kitchen being built before it is fitted, and ask Jo and Sam what to check before you sign a quote. Button: Sign up."
Visual: a photo of the workshop with people in it from last time.
Pitfalls: the time only in the image, an Event with no button, the same Event left up after it has passed.

## Never

- Stock photos, or heavily edited images. Google asks for no significant alterations.
- Keyword-stuffed titles or descriptions.
- Phone numbers in the copy. Use the button.
- Links pasted into the description. Use the button.
- Hashtags of any kind.
- Auto-generated text or copy pasted from the website unchanged.
- Expired Offers or past Events left live.
- Watermarked reposts, or a customer's photo without permission.
- The `tropes` skill's refused phrases: "Welcome to", "Here's the thing", "Whether you're X or Y", "Say goodbye to", "game-changer", "We're passionate about", "Let's dive in", "solutions" as the noun, the "It's not X. It's Y." pivot, the "Fast. Simple. Effective." tricolon, em dashes, invented proof.
